# Visionary Written Style — Google × Apple

How Visionary writes, derived from a verbatim audit of apple.com (iPhone, Mac,
Education, Apple Intelligence, AirPods) and Google surfaces (edu.google.com,
workspace.google.com, Google Store). Apply to every headline, subhead, body
line, CTA, label, chip, and string of microcopy on the public site.

## The blend

- **Apple** gives the voice: short fragments, calm confidence, one crafted
  pun or echo per page, trust stated as a benefit.
- **Google** gives the clarity: sentence case, second person, active voice,
  plain words, verb-first CTAs ("Get started", "Explore …").
- Where they disagree, choose clarity. Never let a pun obscure what the
  product does.

## Rules

0. **Word budgets (measured from apple.com, 2026-09):** hero headline 2–8
   words · hero subhead ≤ 12 · section heading 3–7 · section subhead ≤ 16
   (target 8–14) · card copy ≤ 14 · modal intro ≤ 20 · FAQ answer ≤ 30.
   If a line is over budget, cut — don't compress.
1. **Headlines: 2–8 words, sentence case, period.** Fragments are welcome
   ("Pro further.", "Listening. Remastered."); fragments still get a period.
   Only proper nouns capitalize mid-line. One blue accent span is the maximum.
2. **Every poetic headline gets a plain subhead** — one sentence, second
   person, active voice, ends with a period. Apple pattern: "Might takes
   flight." / "Now supercharged by M5."
3. **Rhythm device: staccato fragments.** 2–3 beats, each a fragment
   ("Thin. Fast. Powerful and portable."). Use once per page, at a natural
   break — never stacked on top of fragments.
4. **Body copy: "you", active voice, short declaratives.** One idea per
   sentence. Prefer 8–20 words. No hedging in marketing lines.
5. **Humanize capability before number.** "All-day battery life" in copy;
   exact figures only where the user needs the number (pricing, specs).
6. **Calm superlatives only.** A flat, absolute claim with a period
   ("The most advanced Mac laptops ever."). No exclamation marks. Anywhere.
7. **No hype vocabulary.** Banned: revolutionary, game-changing, unlock your
   potential, seamless, supercharge, cutting-edge, "X smarter, not harder",
   crack any exam, "reimagined". Confidence is stated as fact.
8. **Trust is a benefit, not a policy.** "Your data. Just where you want it."
   — not "We take privacy seriously."
9. **Personify the product as a helpful actor.** "Visionary keeps going where
   you left off." "It understands what comes next."
10. **CTAs: verb-first, sentence case, 1–4 words.** The set: Get started ·
    Start free · Learn more · See how it works · Explore [thing] · Contact
    sales · Sign in · Sign up. Longer CTAs only to set destination
    expectations ("Explore solutions for educators"). One imperative per
    block. Arrows/chevrons are visual affordances — never typed into labels.
11. **Nav and tab labels: 1–2 words.** Chips and badges: minimal and literal
    ("New", "Available now").
12. **Sentence case everywhere** except proper nouns and product names
    (Visionary, Gemini, Google). Buttons, links, menu items, eyebrows: no
    Title Case ("Get started", never "Get Started"; "Trust & legal" —
    prefer "and" over "&" in headings).
13. **Questions belong in section heads, never heroes** — and only when the
    answer helps ("Which plan is right for you?").
14. **Microcopy is polite, specific, period-ended.** "Please enter a valid
    email address." Form labels may stay Title Case only if the surrounding
    system already does; sentence case is the default.
15. **Reduce em dashes and semicolons.** Prefer a comma, or two sentences.
    One em dash per line at most; an aside is the only justification.
16. **Footnotes carry qualifiers.** Availability, test conditions, and legal
    exceptions live at the bottom so marketing lines stay clean and absolute.
17. **Alt text and aria-labels are sentences, sentence case**, describing what
    the person sees or does — never "image of…".
18. **Consistency beats cleverness.** The same feature is named the same way
    on every page. When in doubt, follow Google: clear, helpful, human.

## Voice checklist before shipping a line

- Could Apple print it without blushing? (fragment, period, no hype)
- Could Google's style guide approve it? (sentence case, you, active voice)
- Does a 12-year-old understand it on one read?
- Would the product actually do what the line promises?
