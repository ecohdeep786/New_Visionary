# FAP-83 end-to-end QA receipt — the public language showcase states the served set

Task: FAP-83 (epic FAP-69, V6 T07 Public Website & Conversion Journey) · Candidate on `zcode/fap-83-public-language-truth` (base verified `origin/beta` @ `f8321b8d` — the FAP-82 merge) · QA executed 2026-11-11 against the candidate dev server (localhost:5173, worktree) and the base checkout server (localhost:5174, beta `f8321b8d`). Edge (msedge channel), Playwright contexts, `--no-proxy-server`.

## Defect (T07 public-site audit, filed as FAP-83)

All five persona pages (`/student`, `/teacher`, `/parent`, `/professional`, `/organization`) shipped a voice-language showcase advertising **six languages** — the orbit dial rendered "Show Tamil", "Show Kannada", "Show Punjabi" controls and the question rows carried full native-script questions for all six — while the deployed product serves exactly **English, Hindi, and Bengali** (`voiceService` maps precisely en/hi/bn to `en-IN`/`hi-IN`/`bn-IN`; the mentor intent router covers en/hi/bn). The overclaim copy sat at the strongest moments of the section:

- shared small print on all five pages: "Listening in Hindi · **understood in every language**" (`PremiumLanguageSection.jsx` line 199);
- section eyebrow default "**Every language**" on four pages plus an explicit `eyebrow="Every language"` on `/professional`;
- `/organization` headline "Your organization. **In every language.**" and a feature card "**In every language.** Learning and reporting in the language each family thinks in."

Measured at beta `f8321b8d` (rendered, 1440×900): every persona page showed 6 chips `[Hindi,English,Bengali,Tamil,Kannada,Punjabi]`, eyebrow "Every language", and the "understood in every language" legend.

Why it matters: a Tamil, Kannada, or Punjabi-speaking visitor is promised a working product in their language at the peak marketing moment, registers, and then finds English, Hindi, and Bengali only — a broken promise at the conversion step, and a direct contradiction of the site's own honest statements (`/accessibility` "Three languages today… with more on the roadmap", `/updates`, `/partners`). This is the public-pages twin of FAP-72 (in-app onboarding language list), which remains a separate ticket owned by a later team.

Reference principle: Apple states feature availability concretely next to aspirational headlines — the "Available in U.S. English" pattern on apple.com feature pages. The vision stays; the availability label tells the truth.
