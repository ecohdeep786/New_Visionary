## Defect (T04 research walk, recorded in Jira on the live product walk)

The workspace dark theme is one remap block in `workspace-design.css`: it rewrites the authored legacy palette classes onto the semantic tokens (`--v-text`/`--v-muted`/`--v-action`/`--v-border`/`--v-soft`/`--v-raised`/…). A class missing from that block keeps its light literal in dark mode. At the verified base, the dashboard inventory held **68 exact palette class tokens across 1,041 source sites**; **30 of those tokens — 265 source sites — were outside the dark remap**. The measured consequence on the Learn journey:

| --- | --- | --- |
| topic "Practice this topic" link (`text-[#0b57d2]`) | `rgb(11,87,210)` on `rgb(25,28,34)` | **2.69:1 — below the product's 4.5:1 informative law (FAP-30)** |
| "All lessons →" and "Learning area" links | `rgb(11,87,210)` | **2.69:1** |

| topic card border (`border-[#dadce0]`) | literal `rgb(218,220,224)` | literal outshines the token border in the dark system (`--v-border` = `#3c4350`) |

This is a cross-cutting token-contract defect, not 265 independent styling bugs: the fix is the dark-only remap extension plus an enforcement test that fails the build if a new legacy token skips the contract. The A/B probe also found one paint the class remap cannot reach — the Learn topic's "Request explanation" is inline `theme.accent`, which stayed `rgb(11,87,210)` at **2.69:1** in dark even after the class remap. That residual is fixed in the same ticket with a dark-yielding accent primitive.

## 2. The change (two source files + one enforcement test)

- `frontend/src/components/dashboard/workspace-design.css`: the dark-only remap gains the **29 missing palette tokens** (text/bg/border/ring/divide/placeholder/disabled/hover families, including the `/20`–`/60` border opacity variants and `bg-[#121317]/5`), each mapped to the semantic token that carries its intent. The 30th token, `bg-[#121317]/40`, stays deliberately unmapped and is documented in the stylesheet as the photography scrim whose dark wash is the legibility device in both color schemes. Two accent primitives are added: `.v-accent-ink` (light keeps `--v-user-accent`; dark resolves to `--v-action`) and `.v-accent-fill` (light fills `--v-user-accent`; dark fills `--v-action` with `--v-on-action` text).
- `frontend/src/components/dashboard/learn/CourseViewer.jsx`: the measured "Request explanation" inline `color: theme.accent` becomes `v-accent-ink` + `--v-user-accent`, and the save-question inline `backgroundColor: theme.accent` becomes `v-accent-fill` + `--v-user-accent`. Light output is unchanged; dark output now follows the token system.
- No copy, flow, field geometry or data change. The 68-token / 1,041-site inventory is unchanged in membership; the only source edits are the CSS remap/primitives and the two paint call sites. All 29 added mappings are inside `@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark)`; the test asserts no palette selector leaks outside it, so light mode keeps the authored literals.
- Scope disclosure: the inline-accent primitive was outside the original class-remap inventory and was found by the AFTER probe. The broader inline-accent population is **not** migrated by FAP-80; FAP-79 Appendix A already discloses the repo-wide 46-match scan across 19 files as outside the class-journey inventory. FAP-80 fixes only the measured Learn residual and establishes the primitive other sites can adopt.

## 6. Reference basis (Apple sources studied in browser, 2026-11-10)

[Apple HIG — Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode): "Dark Mode is a systemwide appearance setting that uses a dark color palette to provide a comfortable viewing experience tailored for low-light environments." … "Ensure that your app looks good in both appearance modes." … "Test your content to make sure that it remains comfortably legible in both appearance modes." … "The color palette in Dark Mode includes dimmer background colors and brighter foreground colors. It's important to realize that these colors aren't necessarily inversions of their light counterparts." … "Embrace colors that adapt to the current appearance." The legacy literals did not adapt; the remap makes them do so.

[Apple HIG — Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color): "Make sure all your app's colors work well in light, dark, and increased contrast contexts." … dynamic colors are "semantically defined by its purpose, rather than its appearance or color values" — the contract now routes 29 legacy literals to purpose tokens instead of repainting them with substitute literals.

[Apple HIG — Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility): "Strive to meet color contrast minimum standards." … the WCAG Level AA guidance table (up to 17 pts 4.5:1; 18 pts / bold 3:1) … "If your app supports Dark Mode, make sure to check the minimum contrast in both light and dark appearances." The probe measures exactly that on both sides. No Apple code or copy reused; no product copy changed.
